    1Type

    1.1Headings and text

    A part opens with its number, large, and its title in spaced capitals. A chapter has its number in spaced italics over a title in bold capitals. Sections below a chapter are numbered and set in bold capitals a size smaller, all of them centred, so the hierarchy is told by case and weight rather than by size, and the page stays an even grey.

    Text runs in one justified column of about seventy characters, hyphenated, with the first line of every paragraph indented and no space between paragraphs. Emphasis is in italics; terms being defined may be bold. Names from a program are set in a typewriter face, as blocksCompressed, and a reference to another part of the document reads as text: see Sec. 2.1 and Fig. 1.

    1Run-in heads

    1. The drawing board. A numbered run-in head starts its paragraph in bold, as the books introduce each item of a list of instruments or methods.

    2. The T-square. The next item follows in the same way, its text continuing on the same line.

    Case 1. An italic run-in head introduces a case or an example within a section.

    Note. A note is set a size smaller, as the books set their remarks on a rule's exceptions.

    1.2Lists and formulae

    A numbered list hangs its numbers in the margin of the column:

    1. Read the length of the field from its first four bytes.
    2. Decompress the rest, and take the values from it in the order they were written.
    3. Look each one up in the list that precedes them.

    Items without an order are dashed:

    • a value per column of the area, for heights;
    • a value per region, for what was generated there.

    A formula is centred on its own line and numbered at the right, and the quantities in it are explained beneath, aligned on their signs:

    n = y · 1024 + z · 32 + x (1)
    where
    n
    the index of a cell in the list;
    x, y, z
    its position within the cube, each from 0 to 31.

    From expression (1) the index of any cell follows directly, and the position from the index by division.

    Sources: Bogolyubov, S., Voinov, A. Engineering Drawing. Moscow: Mir, 1986, pp. 13, 19. · Orlov, P. Fundamentals of Machine Design, vol. 1. Moscow: Mir, 1976, p. 157.

    1.3Tables

    A table is labelled in italics at its top right. It is ruled across the top and the bottom, under its head, and between its columns, never between its rows; its head is set in a small grotesque.

    2Figures

    2.1Drawings

    Figures are drawn in black line of two weights: thick for outlines, thin for dimension lines, hatching and construction. Centre lines are chain lines; hidden edges are dashed. Every word and figure inside a drawing is in GOST drafting lettering, slanted, and dimension lines end in long, narrow arrowheads (Fig. 1).

    Graphs are drawn on a thin grid with their curves thick, the axes and curves lettered like the rest of the drawing, and the description under them (Fig. 2).

    2.2Interactive figures

    A figure that can be worked is drawn like any other. Its values are lettered on lines, as dimensions are; a quantity that varies is set on a scale; a choice between views is a choice between the sub-figure letters; actions are the cells of a title block. The red pencil marks only where you are and what is wrong (Fig. 3).

    Sources: Bogolyubov, S., Voinov, A. Engineering Drawing. Moscow: Mir, 1986, pp. 19, 25–27, 160 (lettering, lines and figures). · Orlov, P. Fundamentals of Machine Design, vol. 1. Moscow: Mir, 1976, p. 277 (graphs).
