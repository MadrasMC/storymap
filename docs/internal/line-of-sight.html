Lines of sight

How the isometric view finds which block to draw on each square of a tile, and why it reads so few chunks. The code is Worker.draw and nearestBlock in src/renderer/isometric.zig.

The slice

The view looks at the world from the south-east, from above. All the blocks drawn on one square of the screen lie on one line, each a block west, north and down from the one before, further from the viewer: a line of sight. Only the first block on it can be seen.

Below is one slice through a made-up world: the blocks where x = z, seen from the side, with x across and the height up. In it, every line of sight is a diagonal going down to the left. The chunks here are 8 blocks on a side, where the game's are 32, and the world is 32 blocks high, where the game's is 256. The explored area is the middle; there are no chunks either side of it.

The steps

  1. Lines. A tile lists the lines whose squares fall on it, about 1,150 at 32-pixel blocks.
  2. From the top. Each line starts at the top of the world and walks down it, a chunk at a time.
  3. Chunks. At each chunk it asks the worker's cache. A chunk not in it is read from the save, turned into one byte per block saying how it's drawn, and kept; a chunk the save doesn't have, or with nothing to draw, is kept as that. A chunk with nothing to draw is passed in one go.
  4. Blocks. In a chunk with blocks, the line checks its blocks one by one. The first one that's drawn is the one on its square; nothing behind it on the line can show, so the line stops there.
  5. Drawing. The block's picture goes on the line's square. Squares of neighbouring lines overlap a little, and the depth buffer keeps the nearer block where they do.

Lines stop near the ground, so chunks deep underground are never read: only those along the explored area's east and south edges, where lines come in from outside and meet the walls. In the game's worlds that's about a fifth of the chunks reading every chunk under each tile took.
