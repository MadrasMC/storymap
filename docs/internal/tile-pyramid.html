The top of the tile pyramid

Why tiles either side of 0 never end up in the same zoomed-out tile, and where the pipeline stops building levels. The code is Pyramid.init in src/tiles/tiles.zig; the pipeline as a whole is in tile-pipeline.html.

    1Parents

    Each zoomed-out level is built from the one below: a tile's parent is its position halved and rounded down, across and down separately. Tiles 0 and 1 share parent 0, tiles 2 and 3 share parent 1, and so on. Rounding down works the same below 0, so tiles −2 and −1 share parent −1.

    So 0 stays 0 and −1 stays −1 at every level. A tile on one side of 0 never shares a parent with a tile on the other: 0 is a wall all the way up. Every multiple of a power of two is a wall for a while: tiles either side of a multiple of 2ⁿ stay apart for n levels. Tiles 7 and 8 sit either side of 8, which is 2³, and meet 4 levels up. 0 is a multiple of every power of two, so its wall never ends.

    2Where the levels stop

    Before: levels were added until one had a single tile. For tiles either side of 0 that never happens, so the render kept adding levels of two tiles until it ran out of memory.

    Now: levels stop at the top level: across and down, the tiles are all at one position, or at exactly −1 and 0. Halving brings neither any closer; any other tiles still come closer further up, even ones on either side of 0 that never meet, such as (−1, 4) and (0, 7), which end side by side at (−1, 1) and (0, 1). Top-down maps, whose tiles are all positive, still end in a single tile; where tiles lie either side of 0, the top level has 2 or 4.

    3Why the isometric view meets it

    Top-down tile numbers in Vintage Story are always positive, around 1,000. The walls the test worlds straddle are multiples of small powers of two, such as 1,000, which is 125 × 8, so their tiles meet a few levels up. The isometric view puts a block at screen x = (x − z) · 8. Vintage Story worlds lie around x = z, so the isometric tiles straddle screen x = 0: tile columns −1 and 0, like "Across x = 0".
