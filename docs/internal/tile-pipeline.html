The tile pipeline

How Storymap turns a save into a pyramid of map tiles, and shares the work between CPU cores. The real code is src/tiles/tiles.zig; rendering.md has the plan.

    1How a render works

    Tiles and levels. The map is drawn as square tiles. Level 0 has full detail. Each tile at level 1 is made from the four tiles under it at level 0, each shrunk to half size and placed in one of its quarters. Level 2 is made from level 1 the same way, and so on up to a single tile showing the whole world.

    With one worker, that's simple: draw a level-0 tile, write it, and shrink it into its parent's buffer. Once a parent has all its quarters, write it and shrink it into its own parent, and so on up. Only one buffer per level is ever needed.

    With several workers, the work has to be shared without them getting in each other's way. So the pyramid is cut at one level, the cut level. Each tile at the cut level, together with every tile under it, is a self-contained part of the job: nothing in it needs anything from another part. The tiles above the cut each need several of these parts, so they're built last.

    2In four points

    1. A subtree is the unit of work. It's one tile at the cut level and every tile under it. Below the cut, work is only ever handed out a whole subtree at a time.

    2. Each subtree belongs to one worker, start to finish. So the worker can shrink each finished tile straight into its parent's buffer, without waiting for or coordinating with any other worker.

    3. The cut is the highest level with at least 4 tiles per worker. That makes between 4 and just under 16 subtrees per worker. Subtrees differ in size, so a worker that finishes early takes the next one instead of sitting idle. In a small world no level has that many tiles, and the cut is at level 0.

    4. Each finished subtree leaves one waiting half: its tile at the cut level, shrunk to half size, 256 KB, a quarter of a 1 MB tile. It waits until the levels above the cut are built, so the waiting halves take about subtrees × 256 KB: with 14 workers, between 14 and 56 MB. The tile list says how many subtrees there are, so that space is set aside before any work starts, along with one more block the size of the level above the cut. The levels above take turns with the two. Separately, each worker keeps one full-size 1 MB buffer per level of its subtree.

    Above the cut, the levels are built one at a time from the waiting halves, with the workers sharing out each level's tiles.

    3The tile list

    Before drawing anything, Storymap lists the level-0 tiles that have something in them: those holding a chunk column with a map chunk. Each level above is the parents of the level below, up to a single tile. Dashed tiles in the pyramid are on the list and not written yet; tiles that aren't on the list are never drawn, and a parent's missing quarters stay transparent. The list is also where the cut comes from: it says how many tiles each level has.
